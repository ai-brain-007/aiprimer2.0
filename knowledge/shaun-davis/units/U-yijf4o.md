## Description

The author turns a bottle of pink lemonade into a pretend stand to show value perception at small scale. A plain cup sells for £1 or £2. Better marketing and an added bag of sweets let the same cup sell for £4.

## Details

How the demonstration runs:
1. Pour a cup of lemonade, a nice full one, a bit more than you first think: you want the customer to be happy.
2. Treat it as a product on your lemonade stand and charge £1 a cup. In real life you would make the stand look better.
3. Marketing is key. A passer-by who sees a nice, beautiful, colourful stand with colourful writing gets the perception it is worth more, so you could charge more.
4. Take a hypothetical price of £2 a cup and apply the two levers: improve the marketing so the perceived value rises; then add a product, a bag of 10 sweets, and sell lemonade plus sweets for £4, double the price.

Ideas the kids added during the game:
- Selling pink lemonade rather than normal lemonade is a bit different, so people who have not tried it may want to.
- Take £10 to the shop, buy fizzy sweets and sell them alongside the drink. The author confirmed this is exactly the right move.

## Notes

The price baseline shifts from £1 in the game to a hypothetical £2 when the author does the doubling maths.
