## Description

Phone and email are for arranging things and gathering information. The close happens face to face, or on Zoom if you cannot meet. People are busier and blunter on the phone; in front of you they have to give you their attention, and once rapport is built the close has far less friction. A landlord who says no on the phone can say yes in the room.

## Details

- On the phone people are busy, cut you off and can be a lot meaner. In person they are friendlier and have no choice but to give you their undivided attention.
- Email is great for finding out more information. Never try to close in the email.
- Order of play: email or phone to arrange, then meet face to face if possible, or a Zoom call if not; build the rapport; then go for the close. With rapport built there is less friction and the close is more seamless.
- A line to move a phone call to a meeting: "When are you in the office?"
- Get from behind the computer and the phone. Physically go and view properties, meet people and build rapport rather than working only from a screen.
- Example: Dave the landlord started very cold and warmed right up during the viewing. On the phone he would have said no. What worked was the basics: having a conversation, building rapport, finding common ground.
