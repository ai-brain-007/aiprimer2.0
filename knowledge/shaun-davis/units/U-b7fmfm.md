## Description

A growing model where single occupants are housed in an HMO and the rent is paid directly by DWP rather than from the council's own budget. Councils like it because it protects their funds while meeting their duty. It carries more risk and more tenant management than the nightly rate, but the author says it tends to make more money.

## Details

- Who: single occupants
- Where: usually an HMO, each tenant with a lockable bedroom, shared facilities, fire regulations in place
- Payer: housing benefit paid directly by DWP, not from the council's pot of money
- Rent: usually the LHA rate or a little above, plus a tenant top-up from Universal Credit
- Incentives from many councils: a cash incentive, a deposit, sometimes two to three months of rent up front
- Workload: more tenants and characters to manage, more housing management than one family
- Risk: the tenant's top-up depends on them managing their income
- Payoff: tends to make more money despite the risk

## Notes

The author does not expand the abbreviations LHA or DWP in this talk.
