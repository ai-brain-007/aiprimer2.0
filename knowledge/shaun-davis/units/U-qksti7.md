## Description

In shared houses where you pay the gas and electric, tenants with a thermostat tend to leave the heating on all day and night. Shaun swaps the thermostat for a receiver: the tenant presses a timer for 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours, and the heating cuts off after that. Nobody has to remember to turn it off, so the boiler is not running while the house is empty or asleep.

## Details

The problem:
- When tenants have access to the thermostat, many leave the heating on 24/7. Shaun walked into a house that felt like a sauna because the boiler temperature was set very high.
- The business pays the gas and electric, not the tenant, so this hits the profitability of that household directly.

The fix:
1. Replace the thermostat with a receiver.
2. The tenant has to manually put the timer on: 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours.
3. After the chosen time the heating cuts off.

Why it works: at night, and in the day when tenants are out, the boiler is not left on unnecessarily. Shaun calls it a game changer.

On inspection: if a house is very hot, check whether it has a receiver fitted.
