## Description

Some investors raise 6 or 12 month finance knowing the project will not finish in time, then raise new money to repay the old investor. Shaun calls it the worst thing you can do: you stack private debt on top of mortgage debt, and if the next raise fails, an investor who trusted the deal goes unpaid.

## Details

- The pattern: short-term investor finance, project runs over, repay by raising more finance from someone else. Some people have built whole portfolios this way.
- Why it is dangerous: you are in massive debt, mortgage debt plus all the private investor debt. Many in the industry end up not paying investors back at all.
- The investor is relying on the deal completing to be repaid from the deal, not on your ability to find another lender.
- Shaun challenged a guest on their podcast who did this; his answer was that he would not be able to sleep at night. The stress of knowing you cannot pay people back can ruin a life.
- Fix: match the loan term to the realistic project timeline with a buffer, or use a JV partnership where nothing has to be repaid.
