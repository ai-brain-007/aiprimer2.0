## Description

Once you have a property the council might take, there is a fixed order of events before tenants move in. You send the property to the council and register on its platform, the surveyor inspects, you fix what they flag, the lease is signed and the council places a family. Move fast, because the agent will want a deposit once your offer goes through, and knowing the order lets you keep everyone moving.

## Details

1. Straight after the viewing, call your council contact: describe the property (for example a four bed in LU3), ask the best way to send it across and how long inspectors take to come out. Luton said about 5 days.
2. Email immediately, from your phone, before the next agent. Do not wait until you are back at a laptop.
   Email template: "Spoke to my colleague earlier about the temporary accommodation. We've got a fantastic property we think may be of interest to you." Then the property address, then "please, can you let us know next steps?"
3. The council replies with links to register on their platform, the agent and landlord approved contractors list.
4. Once you are registered and the landlord has agreed in principle, book the surveyor. They check for damp and similar problems.
5. On the day, ask the surveyor to give you the list of works in a nutshell so you can start before the written report arrives, usually by the next day.
6. The moment the inspector says the property is good to go, send the landlord a commercial lease agreement the same day. The landlord checks it and signs.
7. Sort the trades and complete the remedial works by the agreed date.
8. The surveyor returns to tick everything off.
9. When the property is ready to hand over keys, it passes to the council's property negotiators, who list it and move somebody in. In the Dave deal the family moved in on the Monday after the Friday re-inspection.

Timing caveat: once your offer to the agent goes through, the agent will want a deposit, so you need the council's response before you commit.

## Notes

After the keys, the work is facilitation and leverage. Shaun says you may sometimes be running 10 deals at once and get pulled in every direction; the stress comes with it.
