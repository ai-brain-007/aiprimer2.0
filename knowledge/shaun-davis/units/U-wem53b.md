## Description

Landlords ask for a deposit to cover unpaid rent or damage. Work backwards from those two fears and remove them. Show your finances, show your inspection process, and explain why a company tenant is a lower risk than a normal one.

## Details

1. Understand why a landlord wants a deposit: in case you default on rent or mess up the property
2. Work backwards: how do you make the landlord comfortable that neither will happen
3. Show your financial position
4. Show the processes that keep the property up to standard: a quarterly inspection on every property, either a physical inspection the landlord can attend or an inventory report they sign off
5. Show, do not just tell; proof is everything with someone you are meeting for the first time
6. Remind them you are not a normal tenant: you maintain the property and you guarantee the rent
7. Explain that as a company that does not live there, you do not have the same tenant rights, so you are easier to remove than a tenant
- Watch for the reflex: most landlords have never let this way, treat you as a normal tenant and are used to drilling for a deposit
- Shaun has paid a deposit on about three of all his properties; Michael on none
