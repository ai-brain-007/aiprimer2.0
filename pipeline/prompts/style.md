# Writing style for everything a reader sees (layer 2 cards and pages, layer 3 briefs)

Decided by the owner on 2026-09-27. The approved sample is `docs/style-sample-jab.md`: read it once before
writing, and imitate its voice. Style never changes the content rules: nothing may be added that the source does
not say, and every claim in pipeline output keeps its verified quote.

## The voice

- Talk to the reader as "you", the way a good coach explains something in a chat. Short sentences. Plain words.
- Write for a newcomer first. When a term is needed, give its meaning in the same sentence, once.
- Lead with the point. One idea per paragraph. No filler, no hype, no throat-clearing, no repetition.
- Be concrete: numbers, cues, what to do, what goes wrong, how to practise. Prefer an example over an adjective.
- Layer the depth: a one-line answer, then a paragraph, then the full details, so the reader can stop anywhere.
- Steps are numbered lists. Mistakes come with a fix. Drills come with duration, reps or a measure.
- No em dashes, no parentheses stacked inside sentences, no emojis, no marketing tone, no "in this section we will".

## What it means per field of a card

- `name`: what the author calls it, 2 to 8 words.
- `description`: the one-paragraph explanation you would give a beginner who asked "what is this and why should
  I care?". Two to four short sentences. It clarifies; it does not copy the source.
- `details`: the full teaching, organised so it can be followed: numbered steps for procedures and techniques,
  bullet lists for sets, the numbers as the author gives them, the cues and the conditions ("if you cannot reach,
  step, do not lean"). Complete beats short here, but every line must earn its place.
- `notes`: caveats, prerequisites, what the author warns about, in one to three sentences.

## Small test before you write a description

Would a motivated beginner understand it without opening the source, and could they act on it tomorrow?
If not, rewrite it. If the source itself is vague, say so in `notes` rather than filling the gap yourself.
